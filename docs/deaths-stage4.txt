Stage 4: Loot Hoarder, death snapshots and ordered death waves
=============================================================
Implemented: 2026-10-20
Current catalog: foundation-v4; schema: 1.4
Named resolution policy: DEATH_WAVES_FIFO
Scope: minion Deathrattles built from the supported effect language.

DTH-01: Loot Hoarder is EX1_096, revision foundation-v4, Neutral, 2 mana,
2 Attack, 1 Health, set EXPERT1 and rarity COMMON. Its Deathrattle is DRAW_CARD
count=1, targetPlayer=CONTROLLER_AT_DEATH. The prior nine card definitions are
unchanged. All four catalogs and their original schemas/hashes remain valid.

DTH-02: Schema 1.4 adds DEATHRATTLE, CONTROLLER_AT_DEATH and generic DESTROY,
plus the required deathResolutionPolicy. A minion can have one BATTLECRY and
one DEATHRATTLE; a spell only SPELL_EFFECT; weapons remain unscripted. Reject
duplicate kinds, mismatched mechanic labels, Deathrattles on spells, selected
Deathrattle targets, invalid damage classes, unknown policies and controller-
at-death references outside a Deathrattle. Deathrattles do not ask a player for
a target. Query recipients are allowed for supported non-draw effects.

DTH-03: playOrder is a globally unique, positive, monotonically allocated stamp
when a minion/weapon enters PLAY. State tracks nextPlayOrder. Re-entry through
normal play and transformation receives a fresh stamp. Merely changing a fixture
controller retains the stamp. Normal play assigns actual chronology independently
of entity storage order. Legacy direct-PLAY fixtures without stamps get stamps
once in entity-list order; tests requiring another chronology must supply unique
explicit stamps. Duplicate/invalid stamps and duplicate instance IDs fail closed.
Board position still follows the existing entity-list board representation.

DTH-04: A death checkpoint finds in-play minions whose accumulated damage is
at least max Health or whose pendingDestroy flag is true. Generic DESTROY marks
selected/query MINION recipients pendingDestroy without applying damage or
requiring lethal Health. Ordinary destroy is not prevented by Divine Shield
or damage immunity; selected-target restrictions still apply to declaration.
Hero direct destruction remains unsupported by this new operation.

DTH-05: Before removing any dying minion, sort the batch by playOrder and make
deep snapshots of entity state, controllerAtDeath, board position and eligible
executable Deathrattles. Snapshots retain owner, definition ID/revision,
enchantments, damage and playOrder as last-known information. Then move the whole
batch to GRAVEYARD and append DEATH_BATCH. Removed minions cannot be selected
by later PLAY-zone effects or occupy board slots. Append detached snapshots to
state.deathHistory; mutating a graveyard instance cannot rewrite that record.

DTH-06: Build the wave's immutable Deathrattle queue from those snapshots, then
execute oldest-played first across BOTH players. Active-player priority and
entity-store order do not override explicit playOrder. Emit DEATHRATTLE with
source ID, captured controller and playOrder. Source removal is expected and
does not cancel the queued ability. Its effect controller is the captured
controller, not its owner, active player or a later graveyard value.

DTH-07: Execute all effects in one Deathrattle without a nested death checkpoint.
Finish the current wave's queued Deathrattles before collecting new deaths.
Then snapshot/remove the next batch and resolve its queue in play order.
An older minion dying in a later wave does not jump ahead of the first wave's
remaining sources. Repeat until no lethal/pending-destroy minions remain.
Use an iterative wave loop rather than recursive calls. Zero-health minions
caused mid-wave remain in PLAY until the next checkpoint; query effects may
still see them under the existing area-effect fixture policy.

DTH-08: Battlecries, spells and Deathrattles share _execute_effects. Playing
Loot Hoarder selects no on-play ability and does not draw. Its death does.
DEAL_DAMAGE in a Deathrattle is ABILITY damage, so it receives no Spell Damage
bonus but still uses prevention/Armor/Health rules. DRAW_CARD uses the existing
ordered deck, full-hand reveal/burn, fatigue and per-player counter logic.
EFFECT_CONTROLLER or OPPONENT_OF_EFFECT_CONTROLLER, if used by a synthetic
Deathrattle, also refer to the captured controller context.

DTH-09: A silenced fixture minion contributes no Deathrattle to the queue.
Actual silence effects are not implemented yet; this stage respects the
runtime silenced flag for death snapshot eligibility only. Transform resets
to the destination definition and gets a fresh playOrder without a death.
Burned hand/deck cards and transformed-away identities never create a death
snapshot or Deathrattle. Killing the resulting Sheep has no old Loot ability.

DTH-10: Normal play, attack and draw drain deaths before the final hero result.
Top-level spells/Battlecries now complete their WHOLE ability before that
checkpoint, replacing stage 1's per-effect simplification. Combat still applies
both hits before collection. All queued Deathrattles and nested waves finish
before deciding hero loss/draw, even if an earlier fatigue hit became lethal.
Both players' lethal Deathrattle fatigue can therefore yield a draw under this
fixture policy. Completed matches reject additional checkpoint/actions.

DTH-11: resolve_deaths(state) is a fixture/engine checkpoint, not a free player
action. It returns a copied settled state. Repeating it on an already settled,
unfinished state emits no new deaths or Deathrattles. The internal queue is
local to the synchronous resolution; no pause/resume during a Deathrattle is
offered. Finished states, snapshots and logs remain JSON-serializable.

DTH-12: max_deathrattles is an explicit positive integer prototype configuration,
default 1000 executions per death drain. Exceeding it raises ResolutionLimitError;
the public action commits no partial state, mana, events or result. This is a
simulator safeguard, not a claim that the live game draws on long sequences.
Normal supported operations cannot summon/resurrect, so sources leave play
once per collection and cannot requeue themselves in this stage.

Ordering example
------------------
Wave 1: dying Bomb (playOrder=10) and Loot A (20) are removed together.
Bomb's synthetic Deathrattle damages Loot B (playOrder=1) lethally.
Loot A's already-queued Deathrattle still runs next.
Wave 2: Loot B is now removed and its Deathrattle runs.
Execution order: Bomb, Loot A, Loot B. The old playOrder of Loot B does not
move its later wave ahead of pending work from the first wave.
Bomb is a synthetic test definition only, not a card in the published catalog.

API example (from project root)
--------------------------------
from hearthstone.catalog import load
from hearthstone.prototype import Prototype

engine = Prototype(load())
state = engine.state()
loot = engine.entity('loot-1', 'EX1_096', 0, 'HAND')
top = engine.entity('top-1', 'CS2_182', 0, 'DECK')
state['entities'].extend([loot, top])
state['decks'][0] = ['top-1']
state = engine.play(state, 0, 'loot-1')
# No draw on play. In a fixture, arrange lethal damage:
next(e for e in state['entities'] if e['id'] == 'loot-1')['damage'] = 1
state = engine.resolve_deaths(state)
# loot-1 is in the graveyard; top-1 is in hand; one snapshot was recorded.

In ordinary use, spell damage and combat automatically use this same checkpoint.
No external caller should manually mutate damage in a future player-facing UI.

Tests and traceability
-----------------------
Run: python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
tests/test_deaths.py adds 34 tests (9 catalog, 25 behavioral scenarios).
Full suite: 125 tests, including all 91 prior tests.

DTH-01/02: DeathCatalogTests verifies definition, all four frozen versions,
           ability/label validity, context restrictions, policy and DESTROY.
DTH-03: normal-play chronology; explicit order under reversed entity storage;
        duplicate-stamp failure and transform re-stamping.
DTH-04: pendingDestroy without lethal damage; generic destroy causes next wave.
DTH-05: snapshots preserve owner/controller, board position, definition revision
        and detached modifier data; removed batch cannot be hit again.
DTH-06: simultaneous opposing combat Deathrattles follow global play order;
        captured controller receives the draw; no active-player priority.
DTH-07: nested child waits behind sibling; multiple effects complete first;
        twelve waves settle iteratively without repeated sources.
DTH-08: spell kill draws after DEATH_BATCH; play does not draw; mixed Battlecry
        and Deathrattle dispatch separately; ability damage ignores Spell Damage.
DTH-09: silenced snapshot has no ability; Polymorph and hand burns do not
        trigger old Deathrattle; the resulting Sheep has no old ability.
DTH-10: full-hand Deathrattle burn; ordered escalating fatigue; both queued
        lethal fatigue draws finish before final DRAW result.
DTH-11: idempotent settled checkpoint; JSON serialization/determinism and
        unchanged input state assertions.
DTH-12: explicit budget error; normal play failure preserves mana/history/input.

Death ordering follows an explicit named fixture policy adapted from sections
7 and 12 of the supplied requirements. Those timing observations were flagged
as old/uncertain there. Tests prove the documented prototype behavior, not
current-patch parity. No live-client differential verification was performed.
The synthetic bomb/destroy/chain definitions are in-memory test data, clearly
separate from the ten real card definitions. The twelve-minion chain uses six
minions per side and staggered Health to cause one new death per wave.

This is a death-specific scheduler, not a general event bus. Weapon Deathrattles,
auras/health recalculation, summons/resurrection, duplicate Deathrattles,
Deathrattle doublers, reborn, secrets, on-damage/on-death listeners, state changes
during paused choices and historical exception ordering remain unsupported.
begin_combat_turn remains a readiness fixture primitive, not a full turn runner.
